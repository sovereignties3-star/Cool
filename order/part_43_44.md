EO-SEC-22-001: # EXECUTIVE ORDER: INTEGRITY IN DIGITAL GOVERNANCE AND DECENTRALIZED INFRASTRUCTURE
EO-SEC-22-002: ## SUBCHAPTER XXII - TRANSPARENCY AND SECURITY IN DISTRIBUTED SYSTEMS
EO-SEC-22-003: ### PART 43: THE REAL-TIME BLOCKCHAIN-BASED LOBBYING DISCLOSURE LEDGER
EO-SEC-22-004: #### Section 4301. Establishment of the Distributed Lobbying Ledger (DLL)
EO-SEC-22-005: (a) **Purpose and Authority**: To restore public trust, eliminate dark money influence, and ensure absolute transparency in federal advocacy, there is hereby established the Distributed Lobbying Ledger (DLL). This ledger shall serve as the authoritative, immutable record of all lobbying activities, communications, and financial transactions directed at federal officials.
EO-SEC-22-006: (b) **Technology Stack and Consensus Mechanism**: The DLL shall be implemented utilizing a permissioned, energy-efficient, state-validated distributed ledger technology (DLT) platform. The consensus mechanism must be designed to prevent single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities and shall be maintained by a consortium of neutral federal agencies and academic institutions.
EO-SEC-22-007: (c) **Real-Time Reporting Mandate**: All registered lobbyists, foreign agents, and advocacy groups operating within the jurisdiction of the United States shall log interactions with executive branch officials, members of Congress, and high-level agency staff within twenty-four (24) hours of the contact.
EO-SEC-22-008: (d) **Cryptographic Verification**: Every entry submitted to the DLL must be cryptographically signed using a verified Federal Identity Credential (FIC) linked to the registrant's legal identity, ensuring non-repudiation and absolute accountability.
EO-SEC-22-009: (e) **Public Access and Transparency**: The ledger shall be publicly queryable via decentralized APIs and a user-friendly web interface, ensuring immutable, tamper-proof access to historical lobbying records for journalists, researchers, and the general public.
EO-SEC-22-010: #### Section 4302. Data Standards and Smart Contract Automation
EO-SEC-22-011: (a) **Schema Standardization**: The Office of Management and Budget (OMB), in coordination with the Federal Election Commission (FEC), shall define a standardized JSON-LD schema for all lobbying disclosures to ensure interoperability across federal systems.
EO-SEC-22-012: (b) **Mandatory Fields**: Each transaction entry on the DLL must contain:
EO-SEC-22-013: 1. The unique cryptographic identifier of the lobbyist and their employing organization;
EO-SEC-22-014: 2. The identity, title, and agency of the target federal official;
EO-SEC-22-015: 3. The specific bill, regulation, executive order, or policy action discussed;
EO-SEC-22-016: 4. Any financial contributions, gifts, or assets exchanged, including digital assets; and
EO-SEC-22-017: 5. A cryptographic hash of all written materials, slide decks, or documents presented during the interaction.
EO-SEC-22-018: (c) **Smart Contract Enforcement**: Automated compliance smart contracts shall be deployed on the DLL to flag late filings, incomplete data fields, or anomalous transaction patterns that suggest unregistered lobbying activities.
EO-SEC-22-019: (d) **Automated Penalties**: Failure to register an interaction within the mandated timeframe shall trigger automated administrative fines, scaled to the registrant's annual revenue, executed via smart contract escrow accounts.
EO-SEC-22-020: #### Section 4303. Interoperability and Decentralized Storage
EO-SEC-22-021: (a) **Storage Architecture**: Detailed documentation, presentation decks, and written communications submitted during lobbying sessions shall be stored on a secure, decentralized storage network (e.g., IPFS or Arweave) to prevent data loss or unilateral deletion.
EO-SEC-22-022: (b) **Content Addressing**: All stored files must be referenced on the primary DLL ledger using unique cryptographic content identifiers (CIDs), ensuring that the files cannot be altered post-submission.
EO-SEC-22-023: (c) **Multi-Agency Consensus**: Consensus validation nodes shall be distributed across independent federal entities, including the Government Accountability Office (GAO), the Federal Election Commission (FEC), and the Department of Justice (DOJ), to prevent centralized manipulation.
EO-SEC-22-024: ### PART 44: THE DECENTRALIZED CUSTODY SECURITY ACT
EO-SEC-22-025: #### Section 4401. Protection of Self-Custody Rights and Cryptographic Autonomy
EO-SEC-22-026: (a) **Declaration of Policy**: It is the policy of the United States that the right of individual citizens to conduct self-custody of digital assets is absolute and fundamental to digital privacy, financial sovereignty, and personal security.
EO-SEC-22-027: (b) **Prohibition of Restrictions**: No federal agency, regulatory body, or executive department shall restrict, ban, or penalize the use of self-hosted wallets, hardware wallets, or independent cryptographic key management systems by individuals.
EO-SEC-22-028: (c) **Definition**: "Self-hosted wallet" refers to any software, hardware, or smart contract system allowing an individual to retain exclusive control of their private keys and execute transactions directly on a peer-to-peer network without intermediaries.
EO-SEC-22-029: #### Section 4402. Absolute Prohibition of Federal Backdoors
EO-SEC-22-030: (a) **Backdoor Ban**: No federal agency shall mandate, coerce, or incentivize developers of decentralized custody software, hardware wallets, or cryptographic protocols to implement backdoors, master keys, or escrowed key recovery systems.
EO-SEC-22-031: (b) **Definition of Backdoor**: Any mechanism, protocol vulnerability, or secondary key recovery system designed to bypass user-controlled encryption or allow unauthorized third-party access to private keys or unencrypted transaction data.
EO-SEC-22-032: (c) **Protection of Open-Source Code**: The publication of cryptographic source code, including self-custody wallet software and decentralized protocols, is protected under the First Amendment of the United States Constitution.
EO-SEC-22-033: (d) **Enforcement Restrictions**: No enforcement action, civil asset forfeiture, or criminal prosecution may be taken against developers solely for writing, publishing, or distributing open-source custody code, absent proof of direct criminal intent.
EO-SEC-22-034: #### Section 4403. Security Standards for Institutional Decentralized Custody
EO-SEC-22-035: (a) **Multi-Party Computation (MPC)**: Institutional custodians handling digital assets on behalf of third parties must employ state-of-the-art Multi-Party Computation protocols to eliminate single points of failure in key management.
EO-SEC-22-036: (b) **Hardware Security Modules (HSM)**: All private key share generation, storage, and signing ceremonies must occur within certified, tamper-resistant HSMs meeting FIPS 140-3 Level 4 standards.
EO-SEC-22-037: (c) **Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP)**: Custodial institutions shall utilize zero-knowledge proofs to verify solvency, asset reserves, and compliance with capital requirements without exposing user transaction histories or private balances to the public.
EO-SEC-22-038: (d) **Audit Requirements**: Annual cryptographic audits must be conducted by certified, independent third-party security firms to verify protocol integrity, smart contract safety, and the mathematical soundness of the custody architecture.
EO-SEC-22-039: #### Section 4404. Interagency Coordination and Implementation
EO-SEC-22-040: (a) **Joint Task Force**: There is established the Interagency Task Force on Decentralized Security, co-chaired by the Secretary of the Treasury and the Director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA).
EO-SEC-22-041: (b) **Mandate**: The Task Force shall monitor global threats to decentralized infrastructure, issue guidelines for securing public-key infrastructure, and ensure federal compliance with the self-custody protections established herein.
EO-SEC-22-042: (c) **Reporting**: Within one hundred eighty (180) days of this Order, the Task Force shall submit a comprehensive report to the President on decentralized custody resilience, threat mitigation strategies, and recommendations for international standards.
