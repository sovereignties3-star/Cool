EO-SEC-27-001 # EXECUTIVE ORDER: SECTION 53 AND SECTION 54 - UNIFIED DIGITAL SOVEREIGNTY FRAMEWORK
EO-SEC-27-002 ## PART 53: WARRANTLESS SURVEILLANCE NEUTRALIZATION SWITCH (WSNS)
EO-SEC-27-003 ### Sub-section 53.1: Establishment and Mandate
EO-SEC-27-004 By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the laws of the United States of America, it is hereby ordered that a Warrantless Surveillance Neutralization Switch (WSNS) be established within all federal telecommunications and data processing infrastructure.
EO-SEC-27-005 This mechanism shall serve as an automated, real-time defensive protocol designed to detect, intercept, and neutralize any unauthorized, warrantless domestic surveillance or data harvesting operations conducted by federal, state, or foreign entities.
EO-SEC-27-006 All telecommunications carriers, internet service providers, and cloud hosting platforms operating within the jurisdiction of the United States shall integrate the WSNS API into their core routing and data distribution layers within one hundred and eighty (180) days of this Order.
EO-SEC-27-007 ### Sub-section 53.2: Cryptographic Signature Verification and Auditing
EO-SEC-27-008 To prevent abuse and ensure absolute transparency, any lawful intercept request must be accompanied by a cryptographically signed warrant issued by a court of competent jurisdiction.
EO-SEC-27-009 The WSNS shall utilize a decentralized public key infrastructure (DPKI) to verify the authenticity of all intercept authorization signatures in real-time.
EO-SEC-27-010 Cryptographic verification must employ robust, quantum-resistant signature schemes, specifically utilizing Ed25519 or ECDSA over curve secp256r1, transitioning to post-quantum algorithms (such as ML-DSA) as standardized by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).
EO-SEC-27-011 Every authorization attempt, whether successful or rejected, shall be hashed using SHA-256 and recorded onto an immutable, append-only distributed ledger managed by a multi-agency consortium to prevent retroactive tampering or deletion of access logs.
EO-SEC-27-012 Any data stream access lacking a valid, cryptographically verified signature shall trigger an immediate, automated shutdown of the target data port, isolating the unauthorized tap and alerting the Department of Justice and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.
EO-SEC-27-013 ## PART 54: SAVE AMERICA ACT DIGITAL CONVERSION (SAADC)
EO-SEC-27-014 ### Sub-section 54.1: Transition to Secure Digital Identity
EO-SEC-27-015 In alignment with the Secure and Verifiable Identity (SAVE) America Act, this section directs the immediate modernization of federal identity verification systems, transitioning from legacy physical documentation to secure, privacy-preserving digital credentials.
EO-SEC-27-016 The Department of Homeland Security, in coordination with the Social Security Administration and the National Institute of Standards and Technology, shall establish the Federal Digital Identity Standard (FDIS) within two hundred and seventy (270) days.
EO-SEC-27-017 The FDIS shall mandate the issuance of secure, cryptographically verifiable digital identity tokens to all eligible citizens and lawful residents, designed to integrate seamlessly with state-level systems (such as digital driver's licenses) while maintaining strict federal interoperability.
EO-SEC-27-018 ### Sub-section 54.2: Zero-Knowledge Identity Tokens and Privacy Preservation
EO-SEC-27-019 To protect the fundamental privacy rights of citizens, the SAADC framework shall strictly prohibit the centralized tracking of identity verification events.
EO-SEC-27-020 All digital identity tokens issued under this framework must utilize Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) cryptography, specifically employing Zero-Knowledge Succinct Non-Interactive Arguments of Knowledge (zk-SNARKs) or Zero-Knowledge Scalable Transparent Arguments of Knowledge (zk-STARKs).
EO-SEC-27-021 These zero-knowledge identity tokens shall allow citizens to prove specific attributes (such as citizenship, age, or employment eligibility) to third-party verifiers without disclosing their underlying personally identifiable information (PII), Social Security numbers, or exact dates of birth.
EO-SEC-27-022 The cryptographic protocol must ensure that the identity issuer (the federal government) cannot track where, when, or for what purpose a citizen presents their zero-knowledge identity token, thereby eliminating the risk of state-sponsored behavioral profiling.
EO-SEC-27-023 All verification transactions shall be executed locally on the user's device using secure enclave hardware, ensuring that private keys never leave the user's physical possession.
EO-SEC-27-024 ## PART 55: INTEGRATION AND ENFORCEMENT
EO-SEC-27-025 ### Sub-section 55.1: Interoperability of WSNS and SAADC
EO-SEC-27-026 The cryptographic infrastructure established under Part 53 (WSNS) and Part 54 (SAADC) shall share a unified security architecture to maximize efficiency and minimize the federal cyber-attack surface.
EO-SEC-27-027 The zero-knowledge identity tokens developed under Part 54 shall be utilized by system administrators and law enforcement officers to authenticate their access to the WSNS auditing interface, ensuring that even administrative access is fully audited without compromising the privacy of the administrators.
EO-SEC-27-028 ### Sub-section 55.2: Penalties and Compliance
EO-SEC-27-029 Any federal agency, officer, or private contractor found to be in violation of the provisions of this Order, including the bypass of the WSNS or the unauthorized collection of PII in violation of the SAADC, shall be subject to immediate administrative, civil, and criminal penalties.
EO-SEC-27-030 The Attorney General shall establish a dedicated task force to monitor compliance, conduct regular penetration testing of the WSNS, and audit the cryptographic integrity of the zero-knowledge identity token issuance systems.
EO-SEC-27-031 This Order is not intended to, and does not, create any right or benefit, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity by any party against the United States, its departments, agencies, or entities, its officers, employees, or agents, or any other person.
