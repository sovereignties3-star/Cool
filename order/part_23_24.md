EO-SEC-12-001 # EXECUTIVE ORDER: NATIONAL SECURITY AND FINANCIAL INTEGRITY IN THE QUANTUM ERA
EO-SEC-12-002 ## PART 23: POST-QUANTUM SIGNATURE STANDARD (PQSS)
EO-SEC-12-003 ### Section 2301. Purpose and Scope of Quantum-Resistant Cryptography
EO-SEC-12-004 This Section establishes the Post-Quantum Signature Standard (PQSS) to safeguard federal information systems, national security assets, and critical infrastructure against the cryptographic threat posed by cryptanalytically relevant quantum computers (CRQCs).
EO-SEC-12-005 The rapid advancement of quantum computing technologies necessitates an immediate, structured transition from legacy public-key cryptography—specifically RSA, ECDSA, and Diffie-Hellman—to mathematically secure lattice-based cryptographic algorithms.
EO-SEC-12-006 Failure to transition critical infrastructure to post-quantum standards exposes the United States to "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks, wherein adversaries intercept and store encrypted federal data to decrypt once quantum capabilities mature.
EO-SEC-12-007 ### Section 2302. Mandated Cryptographic Algorithms and Standards
EO-SEC-12-008 (a) All executive departments and agencies shall transition their digital signature infrastructures to primary lattice-based algorithms approved by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).
EO-SEC-12-009 (1) ML-DSA (Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm, formerly CRYSTALS-Dilithium) is hereby designated as the primary standard for general-purpose digital signatures, identity verification, and code signing.
EO-SEC-12-010 (2) FN-DSA (Fast-Fourier Lattice-Based Digital Signature Scheme, formerly Falcon) shall be deployed in environments requiring highly constrained bandwidth or minimal signature size.
EO-SEC-12-011 (3) Stateful Hash-Based Signature schemes, specifically LMS (Leighton-Micali Signatures) and XMSS (eXtended Merkle Signature Scheme), are authorized for software updates, firmware validation, and early-stage bootloader security.
EO-SEC-12-012 (b) The use of non-quantum-resistant signature schemes for new federal deployments is strictly prohibited unless granted a temporary waiver by the Federal Chief Information Officer (CIO) in consultation with the National Security Agency (NSA).
EO-SEC-12-013 ### Section 2303. Implementation Timelines and Agency Responsibilities
EO-SEC-12-014 (a) Within 180 days of this Order, the head of each agency shall conduct a comprehensive inventory of all information technology systems utilizing public-key cryptography for digital signatures, authentication, or non-repudiation.
EO-SEC-12-015 (b) By Fiscal Year 2026, all newly procured federal information systems must support native post-quantum signature verification.
EO-SEC-12-016 (c) By Fiscal Year 2030, all legacy federal systems must complete full migration to PQSS-compliant algorithms, rendering all non-quantum-resistant signature schemes obsolete within federal networks.
EO-SEC-12-017 (d) The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the National Security Agency (NSA) shall jointly publish implementation guidance, migration playbooks, and interoperability profiles for hybrid classical-quantum signature modes.
EO-SEC-12-018 ### Section 2304. Research and Development of Advanced Lattice-Based Cryptography
EO-SEC-12-019 (a) The National Science Foundation (NSF) and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) shall establish a joint research initiative to study the long-term security of lattice-based assumptions.
EO-SEC-12-020 (1) Research shall focus on the hardness of the Learning With Errors (LWE) problem and its algebraic variants, including Ring-LWE and Module-LWE.
EO-SEC-12-021 (2) Investigations shall be conducted into potential quantum algorithms capable of solving lattice reduction problems, such as the Shortest Vector Problem (SVP) and Closest Vector Problem (CVP).
EO-SEC-12-022 (3) Funding shall be prioritized for the development of side-channel resistant implementations of ML-DSA and FN-DSA to prevent physical attacks on cryptographic hardware modules.
EO-SEC-12-023 ## PART 24: CLOSED-LOOP MATRIX AUDIT (CLMA)
EO-SEC-12-024 ### Section 2401. Purpose and Scope of Automated Financial Auditing
EO-SEC-12-025 This Section establishes the Closed-Loop Matrix Audit (CLMA) framework to modernize corporate financial oversight, eliminate systemic reporting fraud, and automate the verification of corporate balance sheets.
EO-SEC-12-026 By transitioning from periodic, sample-based manual audits to continuous, automated, and cryptographically verifiable ledger reconciliation, the federal government shall ensure unprecedented transparency and stability in public markets.
EO-SEC-12-027 The CLMA framework is designed to prevent corporate malfeasance, reduce systemic risk in the financial sector, and provide real-time visibility into the solvency of systemically important financial institutions (SIFIs).
EO-SEC-12-028 ### Section 2402. Technical Architecture of the Closed-Loop Matrix
EO-SEC-12-029 (a) The CLMA system shall utilize a multi-dimensional, double-entry ledger matrix that continuously reconciles inter-corporate transactions across participating entities.
EO-SEC-12-030 (1) Real-Time Ledger Reconciliation: All transactions between registered corporations must be reported to a secure, decentralized, or federated ledger system managed under the oversight of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).
EO-SEC-12-031 (2) Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs): To protect proprietary business intelligence, trade secrets, and consumer privacy, the CLMA shall employ zero-knowledge proofs (specifically zk-SNARKs) to verify transaction validity, asset ownership, and balance sheet solvency without disclosing underlying transaction details or counterparty identities to unauthorized entities.
EO-SEC-12-032 (3) Automated API Integration: Corporations shall expose secure, standardized, read-only APIs to authorized regulatory auditing engines to facilitate continuous, real-time extraction of balance sheet metrics, income statements, and cash flow data.
EO-SEC-12-033 ### Section 2403. Regulatory Enforcement and Compliance Mandates
EO-SEC-12-034 (a) The SEC, in coordination with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) and the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), shall promulgate rules requiring all publicly traded corporations and financial institutions with assets exceeding $500 million to integrate their internal accounting systems with the CLMA framework.
EO-SEC-12-035 (b) Continuous Compliance: Traditional annual and quarterly reporting cycles (Forms 10-K and 10-Q) shall be augmented by continuous, automated compliance scoring generated by the CLMA matrix.
EO-SEC-12-036 (c) Anomaly Detection and Enforcement: The CLMA system shall run automated machine learning models to detect balance sheet anomalies, circular transactions, wash trading, and off-balance-sheet liabilities. Upon detection of a high-confidence anomaly, the system shall automatically issue a non-compliance alert to the SEC Enforcement Division and freeze the affected entity's automated regulatory clearance.
EO-SEC-12-037 ### Section 2404. Interoperability and Security of the Audit Infrastructure
EO-SEC-12-038 (a) The CLMA infrastructure must be secured using the Post-Quantum Signature Standard (PQSS) established under Part 23 of this Order to prevent unauthorized decryption or tampering of financial records by quantum-capable adversaries.
EO-SEC-12-039 (b) The Department of the Treasury, in consultation with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), shall establish strict cryptographic key management protocols for all automated auditing APIs and zero-knowledge proof generation systems.
EO-SEC-12-040 (c) All data transmitted within the CLMA framework must be encrypted in transit and at rest using algorithms that meet or exceed the requirements of the Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS) for post-quantum security.
