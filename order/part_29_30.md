EO-SEC-15-001 | # EXECUTIVE ORDER: SECTION 29 & 30 - INTEGRATED TECH POLICY & DEVELOPER SAFE HARBORS
EO-SEC-15-002 | 
EO-SEC-15-003 | ## PART 29: THE THUNE-SCHUMER PIVOT DIRECTIVE
EO-SEC-15-004 | 
EO-SEC-15-005 | ### Section 29.1: Purpose and Senate Leadership Dynamics
EO-SEC-15-006 | This Directive establishes a formalized, high-priority coordination mechanism between the Executive Branch
EO-SEC-15-007 | and the leadership of the United States Senate, specifically recognizing the strategic bipartisan dynamics
EO-SEC-15-008 | represented by Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer and Senate Republican Leader John Thune. The rapid
EO-SEC-15-009 | evolution of frontier technologies—including artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and decentralized
EO-SEC-15-010 | ledger architectures—demands a unified legislative-executive pivot. This directive ensures that executive
EO-SEC-15-011 | agencies align their regulatory postures with the emerging bipartisan consensus in the Senate, minimizing
EO-SEC-15-012 | regulatory friction and accelerating the passage of comprehensive, pro-innovation statutory frameworks.
EO-SEC-15-013 | 
EO-SEC-15-014 | ### Section 29.2: Establishment of the Joint Senate-Executive Technology Pivot Taskforce (JSETPT)
EO-SEC-15-015 | (a) There is hereby established an interagency taskforce to be known as the Joint Senate-Executive Technology
EO-SEC-15-016 | Pivot Taskforce. The Taskforce shall be co-chaired by the Director of the Office of Science and Technology
EO-SEC-15-017 | Policy (OSTP) and a designated senior representative of the Senate Leadership Liaison Office.
EO-SEC-15-018 | (b) The Taskforce shall facilitate bi-weekly briefings with the offices of the Senate Majority Leader, the
EO-SEC-15-019 | Senate Minority Leader, and the Chairs and Ranking Members of the Senate Committees on Commerce, Science,
EO-SEC-15-020 | and Transportation, and Finance.
EO-SEC-15-021 | (c) The primary mandate of the Taskforce is to synchronize executive rulemaking with the legislative priorities
EO-SEC-15-022 | outlined in the Thune-Schumer bipartisan tech framework, ensuring that federal agencies do not preemptively
EO-SEC-15-023 | stifle emerging sectors before Congress can enact permanent statutory definitions.
EO-SEC-15-024 | 
EO-SEC-15-025 | ### Section 29.3: Strategic Legislative Synchronization
EO-SEC-15-026 | To prevent regulatory fragmentation, all executive agencies—including the Federal Trade Commission (FTC),
EO-SEC-15-027 | the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)—shall submit
EO-SEC-15-028 | a quarterly "Regulatory Alignment Report" to the Taskforce. This report must detail how proposed rules
EO-SEC-15-029 | align with the legislative intent of pending bipartisan Senate bills, specifically focusing on consensus
EO-SEC-15-030 | standards for risk mitigation, data privacy, and international competitiveness.

EO-SEC-15-032 | ## PART 30: THE LUMMIS DEVELOPER PROTECTION UPGRADE
EO-SEC-15-033 | 
EO-SEC-15-034 | ### Section 30.1: Purpose and Statutory Safe Harbors
EO-SEC-15-035 | In alignment with the legislative initiatives championed by Senator Cynthia Lummis, this Part establishes
EO-SEC-15-036 | robust, clear, and enforceable statutory safe harbors for independent software developers, protocol
EO-SEC-15-037 | architects, and non-custodial network participants within the United States. The purpose of this upgrade
EO-SEC-15-038 | is to protect the fundamental right to write, publish, and maintain open-source code, ensuring that
EO-SEC-15-039 | software development is recognized as a protected form of expression under the First Amendment.
EO-SEC-15-040 | 
EO-SEC-15-041 | ### Section 30.2: Definition of Non-Custodial Developer
EO-SEC-15-042 | For the purposes of this Executive Order, a "Non-Custodial Developer" is defined as any individual or
EO-SEC-15-043 | entity that writes, designs, tests, or publishes software code, smart contracts, or decentralized protocols,
EO-SEC-15-044 | provided that such individual or entity does not:
EO-SEC-15-045 | (i) Take physical or digital custody of user funds, assets, or private keys;
EO-SEC-15-046 | (ii) Possess unilateral authority to execute transactions on behalf of any third party; or
EO-SEC-15-047 | (iii) Maintain direct control over the operational execution of deployed decentralized networks.
EO-SEC-15-048 | 
EO-SEC-15-049 | ### Section 30.3: Exclusion from Financial Intermediary Classifications
EO-SEC-15-050 | (a) No federal agency, including the Department of the Treasury, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network
EO-SEC-15-051 | (FinCEN), or the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), shall classify a Non-Custodial Developer as a "money
EO-SEC-15-052 | transmitter," "broker," "dealer," "financial institution," or "custodian" solely on the basis of their
EO-SEC-15-053 | development, publication, or maintenance of open-source software code.
EO-SEC-15-054 | (b) The act of writing and publishing code does not constitute the provision of a financial service. Federal
EO-SEC-15-055 | regulatory actions must target the illicit actors who utilize software for unlawful purposes, rather than
EO-SEC-15-056 | the independent architects who authored the underlying, neutral technology.
EO-SEC-15-057 | 
EO-SEC-15-058 | ### Section 30.4: Implementation and Enforcement Protections
EO-SEC-15-059 | The Department of Justice (DOJ), in coordination with the Department of Commerce, shall issue formal
EO-SEC-15-060 | guidance to all federal law enforcement agencies within 60 days of this Order, establishing strict
EO-SEC-15-061 | evidentiary standards before any legal action or investigation is initiated against software developers.
EO-SEC-15-062 | This guidance shall explicitly prohibit the use of regulatory enforcement actions as a tool to compel
EO-SEC-15-063 | developers to insert backdoors, alter open-source protocols, or act as surveillance agents for the state.
EO-SEC-15-064 | 
EO-SEC-15-065 | ### Section 30.5: Interagency Coordination and Global Standards
EO-SEC-15-066 | The Secretary of State, in consultation with the United States Trade Representative (USTR), shall advocate
EO-SEC-15-067 | for the international adoption of these developer safe harbors in all bilateral and multilateral trade
EO-SEC-15-068 | negotiations, ensuring that American software architects remain globally competitive and protected from
EO-SEC-15-069 | extraterritorial regulatory overreach.
