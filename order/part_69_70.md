EO-SEC-35-001 # EXECUTIVE ORDER: SOVEREIGN DATA AND LOGISTICAL EFFICIENCY
EO-SEC-35-002 ## PART 69: SOVEREIGN PERSONAL DATA DECLARATION
EO-SEC-35-003 ### Section 69.1: Declaration of Inalienable Data Property Rights
EO-SEC-35-004 This declaration establishes that all personal data generated by, or associated with, an individual citizen constitutes their private, inalienable property.
EO-SEC-35-005 Under this framework, individuals possess absolute ownership, control, and sovereignty over their digital footprint, biometric profiles, and behavioral metadata.
EO-SEC-35-006 The legal doctrine of self-sovereign identity (SSI) is hereby integrated into federal administrative procedures, superseding legacy data-custody models.
EO-SEC-35-007 No commercial entity, government agency, or third-party intermediary may harvest, store, analyze, or monetize personal data without explicit, revocable consent.
EO-SEC-35-008 Consent must be obtained via cryptographically signed, granular agreements that clearly define the scope, duration, and purpose of data utilization.
EO-SEC-35-009 ### Section 69.2: Decentralized Data Vaults and Technical Standards
EO-SEC-35-010 To operationalize sovereign data rights, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) shall define protocols for Decentralized Data Vaults (DDVs).
EO-SEC-35-011 DDVs shall serve as secure, user-controlled repositories utilizing zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to verify attributes without disclosing underlying raw data.
EO-SEC-35-012 Federal agencies are directed to transition all public-facing identity verification systems to support decentralized, interoperable W3C Verifiable Credentials.
EO-SEC-35-013 Any legacy centralized database containing personally identifiable information (PII) must be systematically phased out or refactored into decentralized architectures.
EO-SEC-35-014 This transition mitigates systemic cybersecurity risks by eliminating centralized honeypots of sensitive citizen information, reducing national vulnerability.
EO-SEC-35-015 ### Section 69.3: Legal Remedies and Enforcement Mechanisms
EO-SEC-35-016 The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) is granted expanded jurisdiction to prosecute unauthorized data exploitation as a violation of fundamental property rights.
EO-SEC-35-017 Citizens shall possess a private right of action to seek statutory damages against any entity violating the sovereign data provisions established herein.
EO-SEC-35-018 Punitive damages shall scale proportionally with the volume of unauthorized data processed, ensuring a robust deterrent against corporate non-compliance.
EO-SEC-35-019 ## PART 70: LOGISTICAL COST ELIMINATION PROOF
EO-SEC-35-020 ### Section 70.1: Mandate for Cryptographic Administrative Automation
EO-SEC-35-021 This section establishes the Logistical Cost Elimination Proof, a binding mandate to reduce federal administrative overhead by a minimum of eighty percent.
EO-SEC-35-022 All federal procurement, supply chain tracking, and inter-agency resource allocation shall transition to automated, cryptographically verified systems.
EO-SEC-35-023 By utilizing distributed ledger technology and automated smart contracts, the need for manual auditing, reconciliation, and intermediary verification is eliminated.
EO-SEC-35-024 Smart contracts shall execute payments automatically upon the cryptographic verification of delivery milestones, reducing transaction latency to near-zero.
EO-SEC-35-025 ### Section 70.2: Deconstruction of Bureaucratic Redundancy
EO-SEC-35-026 The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) shall conduct an immediate audit of all multi-layered administrative approval workflows across federal agencies.
EO-SEC-35-027 Any workflow requiring more than two levels of manual administrative sign-off must be refactored into a single-layer automated validation protocol.
EO-SEC-35-028 Legacy paper-based documentation, manual data entry, and redundant reporting structures are hereby declared obsolete and shall be decommissioned.
EO-SEC-35-029 Agencies must implement zero-trust logistical pipelines where data integrity is guaranteed at the point of origin through hardware-secured cryptographic keys.
EO-SEC-35-030 This shift eliminates administrative friction, prevents fraudulent billing, and ensures real-time visibility into the allocation of public resources.
EO-SEC-35-031 ### Section 70.3: Cost-Elimination Metrics and Reporting
EO-SEC-35-032 Each agency shall publish a quarterly Logistical Cost Elimination Proof, detailing the exact reduction in administrative hours and operational expenditures.
EO-SEC-35-033 These reports must be cryptographically signed by the agency's Chief Financial Officer and published to a publicly accessible, immutable ledger.
EO-SEC-35-034 Failure to meet the prescribed cost-reduction benchmarks shall trigger automatic budgetary reallocations toward decentralized infrastructure projects.
EO-SEC-35-035 The savings realized through these logistical efficiencies shall be directly reallocated to fund the deployment of the sovereign data systems defined in Part 69.
EO-SEC-35-036 ## PART 69 & 70 UNIFICATION PROVISION
EO-SEC-35-037 ### Section 71.1: The Sovereign Data and Logistical Efficiency Nexus
EO-SEC-35-038 Parts 69 and 70 are hereby unified into a single operational framework linking personal data sovereignty directly with administrative cost reduction.
EO-SEC-35-039 By utilizing self-sovereign identity credentials (Part 69), the federal government eliminates the administrative overhead of identity management (Part 70).
EO-SEC-35-040 Citizens interacting with federal services shall authenticate using their private DDVs, removing the need for agency-side data storage and verification staff.
EO-SEC-35-041 This integration ensures that data privacy and logistical efficiency are not competing priorities, but mutually reinforcing pillars of modern governance.
EO-SEC-35-042 The unified system guarantees that citizen privacy is absolute, while administrative costs are mathematically proven to approach absolute zero.
