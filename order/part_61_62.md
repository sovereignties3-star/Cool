EO-SEC-31-001 | # EXECUTIVE ORDER: SECURING THE DOMESTIC DIGITAL PERIMETER AND CITIZEN COGNITIVE INTEGRITY
EO-SEC-31-002 | 
EO-SEC-31-003 | ## SUBCHAPTER XXXI - INTEGRATED DEFENSE SYSTEMS
EO-SEC-31-004 | ### PART 61: FOREIGN ADVERSARY NETWORK ISOLATION (FANI)
EO-SEC-31-005 | ### PART 62: BEHAVIORAL MANIPULATION DEFLECTION LAYER (BMDL)
EO-SEC-31-006 | 
EO-SEC-31-007 | By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the laws of the United States of America,
EO-SEC-31-008 | including the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (50 U.S.C. 1701 et seq.), the National Emergencies Act
EO-SEC-31-009 | (50 U.S.C. 1601 et seq.), and section 301 of title 3, United States Code, it is hereby ordered as follows:
EO-SEC-31-010 | 
EO-SEC-31-011 | #### SECTION 1. POLICY AND DECLARATION OF PRINCIPLES
EO-SEC-31-012 | 
EO-SEC-31-013 | (a) The digital infrastructure of the United States is under continuous, sophisticated assault by foreign adversaries
EO-SEC-31-014 | seeking to compromise national security, steal intellectual property, and manipulate public discourse.
EO-SEC-31-015 | (b) To counter these threats, the Nation must establish a unified defense architecture that combines robust physical
EO-SEC-31-016 | and logical network isolation with advanced cryptographic and behavioral deflection mechanisms.
EO-SEC-31-017 | (c) This Order establishes a dual-layer defense system: the Foreign Adversary Network Isolation (FANI) framework
EO-SEC-31-018 | to sever hostile routing pathways, and the Behavioral Manipulation Deflection Layer (BMDL) to protect the digital
EO-SEC-31-019 | identities and cognitive autonomy of United States citizens from foreign algorithmic exploitation.
EO-SEC-31-020 | 
EO-SEC-31-021 | #### SECTION 2. PART 61: FOREIGN ADVERSARY NETWORK ISOLATION (FANI)
EO-SEC-31-022 | 
EO-SEC-31-023 | (a) **Establishment of the National Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) Shield:**
EO-SEC-31-024 |     (i) The Department of Homeland Security (DHS), in coordination with the Federal Communications Commission (FCC),
EO-SEC-31-025 |     shall mandate the implementation of secure BGP routing protocols across all Tier-1 and Tier-2 internet service
EO-SEC-31-026 |     providers (ISPs) operating within the territorial jurisdiction of the United States.
EO-SEC-31-027 |     (ii) All domestic ISPs must implement Route Origin Validation (ROV) using Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI)
EO-SEC-31-028 |     to prevent BGP hijacking and unauthorized route redirection by foreign state actors.
EO-SEC-31-029 | 
EO-SEC-31-030 | (b) **Dynamic IP Blacklisting and Autonomous System Number (ASN) Isolation:**
EO-SEC-31-031 |     (i) The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) shall maintain a real-time, cryptographically
EO-SEC-31-032 |     signed registry of hostile IP address blocks, subnets, and ASNs associated with foreign adversary nations.
EO-SEC-31-033 |     (ii) Domestic telecommunications carriers are required to drop all inbound and outbound traffic originating from
EO-SEC-31-034 |     or destined for the blacklisted entities designated in the CISA registry within sixty (60) seconds of registry updates.
EO-SEC-31-035 |     (iii) Research on IP blacklisting indicates that static lists are insufficient; therefore, CISA must deploy
EO-SEC-31-036 |     machine-learning-driven, predictive IP blacklisting models that analyze transient routing anomalies and block
EO-SEC-31-037 |     adversarial proxy networks before they can be utilized for coordinated cyber campaigns.
EO-SEC-31-038 | 
EO-SEC-31-039 | (c) **Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) and Perimeter Filtering:**
EO-SEC-31-040 |     (i) All international subsea cable landing stations operating within United States waters shall install advanced,
EO-SEC-31-041 |     hardware-accelerated DPI systems capable of analyzing traffic at line-rate speeds without introducing latency.
EO-SEC-31-042 |     (ii) These systems shall be configured to detect and neutralize malicious payloads, command-and-control (C2)
EO-SEC-31-043 |     communications, and unauthorized data exfiltration vectors linked to foreign intelligence services.
EO-SEC-31-044 | 
EO-SEC-31-045 | #### SECTION 3. PART 62: BEHAVIORAL MANIPULATION DEFLECTION LAYER (BMDL)
EO-SEC-31-046 | 
EO-SEC-31-047 | (a) **Mitigation of Algorithmic Profiling and Cognitive Warfare:**
EO-SEC-31-048 |     (i) Foreign adversaries utilize sophisticated metadata harvesting to construct behavioral profiles of United States
EO-SEC-31-049 |     citizens, enabling targeted disinformation, psychological manipulation, and social destabilization campaigns.
EO-SEC-31-050 |     (ii) The BMDL is hereby established as a mandatory national standard to disrupt the collection of behavioral
EO-SEC-31-051 |     telemetry by hostile foreign platforms and their domestic intermediaries.
EO-SEC-31-052 | 
EO-SEC-31-053 | (b) **Masking Citizen Metadata Signatures:**
EO-SEC-31-054 |     (i) The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), in collaboration with the Defense Advanced Research
EO-SEC-31-055 |     Projects Agency (DARPA), shall define the Federal Metadata Obfuscation Standard (FMOS).
EO-SEC-31-056 |     (ii) FMOS shall require all consumer-facing digital services and network operators to implement metadata masking
EO-SEC-31-057 |     techniques, including packet-size padding, traffic timing randomization, and dummy packet injection (chaffing).
EO-SEC-31-058 |     (iii) Research demonstrates that masking citizen metadata signatures prevents adversarial machine learning models
EO-SEC-31-059 |     from performing traffic analysis, device fingerprinting, and user correlation, thereby neutralizing the utility
EO-SEC-31-060 |     of intercepted network flows.
EO-SEC-31-061 |     (iv) All operating systems and web browsers distributed within the United States must integrate native,
EO-SEC-31-062 |     low-overhead metadata obfuscation layers that mask DNS queries (via DNS-over-HTTPS with randomized padding)
EO-SEC-31-063 |     and TLS handshake SNI (Server Name Indication) fields through Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) protocols.
EO-SEC-31-064 | 
EO-SEC-31-065 | (c) **Differential Privacy and Noise Injection:**
EO-SEC-31-066 |     (i) Large-scale data brokers operating within the United States must apply mathematical differential privacy
EO-SEC-31-067 |     guarantees to all exported or shared datasets containing citizen behavioral, demographic, or location data.
EO-SEC-31-068 |     (ii) The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) shall enforce strict limits on the granularity of behavioral telemetry,
EO-SEC-31-069 |     requiring the injection of synthetic noise to prevent the reconstruction of individual citizen identities by
EO-SEC-31-070 |     foreign intelligence data-mining operations.
EO-SEC-31-071 | 
EO-SEC-31-072 | #### SECTION 4. IMPLEMENTATION, COMPLIANCE, AND ENFORCEMENT
EO-SEC-31-073 | 
EO-SEC-31-074 | (a) **Interagency Task Force on Digital Sovereignty:**
EO-SEC-31-075 |     (i) There is established an Interagency Task Force on Digital Sovereignty, co-chaired by the National Security
EO-SEC-31-076 |     Advisor, the Director of National Intelligence, and the Secretary of Homeland Security.
EO-SEC-31-077 |     (ii) The Task Force shall oversee the deployment of the FANI and BMDL frameworks, ensuring seamless integration
EO-SEC-31-078 |     between perimeter network defense and endpoint metadata protection.
EO-SEC-31-079 | 
EO-SEC-31-080 | (b) **Compliance Timelines and Penalties:**
EO-SEC-31-081 |     (i) Tier-1 ISPs and major telecommunications providers must achieve full compliance with Part 61 (FANI)
EO-SEC-31-082 |     requirements within one hundred and eighty (180) days of the issuance of this Order.
EO-SEC-31-083 |     (ii) Software developers, operating system vendors, and data brokers must implement Part 62 (BMDL) standards
EO-SEC-31-084 |     within two hundred and seventy (270) days of the publication of the final FMOS guidelines by NIST.
EO-SEC-31-085 |     (iii) Non-compliance shall result in civil penalties of up to $1,000,000 per day per violation, and persistent
EO-SEC-31-086 |     failure to comply may result in the revocation of operating licenses within the United States.
EO-SEC-31-087 | 
EO-SEC-31-088 | #### SECTION 5. DEFINITIONS
EO-SEC-31-089 | 
EO-SEC-31-090 | (a) "Foreign Adversary" means any foreign government or foreign non-government person engaged in a long-term pattern
EO-SEC-31-091 | or serious instances of conduct significantly adverse to the national security of the United States or its allies.
EO-SEC-31-092 | (b) "Metadata Signature" means the structural, temporal, and quantitative characteristics of digital communications,
EO-SEC-31-093 | including but not limited to packet sizes, transmission intervals, routing paths, and protocol headers, which
EO-SEC-31-094 | collectively identify a unique user, device, or behavioral pattern without relying on payload content.
EO-SEC-31-095 | (c) "IP Blacklisting" means the systematic blocking of network traffic to and from specific Internet Protocol
EO-SEC-31-096 | addresses or ranges determined to be hostile, compromised, or controlled by foreign adversaries.
EO-SEC-31-097 | (d) "Chaffing" means the transmission of spurious, non-informative data packets designed to obscure the timing,
EO-SEC-31-098 | volume, and destination of legitimate user traffic from passive network surveillance.
