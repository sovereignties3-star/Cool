EO-SEC-06-001 # EXECUTIVE ORDER: UNIFIED FRAMEWORK ON FINANCIAL-GRADE API CONFORMANCE AND MACHINE-TO-MACHINE SETTLEMENT EQUIVALENCE
EO-SEC-06-002 
EO-SEC-06-003 ## PREAMBLE
EO-SEC-06-004 By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the laws of the United States of America,
EO-SEC-06-005 and in order to secure the national financial infrastructure, promote automated commerce, and establish
EO-SEC-06-006 rigorous cryptographic standards for machine-to-machine transactions, it is hereby ordered as follows:
EO-SEC-06-007 
EO-SEC-06-008 ## PART I: THE FINANCIAL-GRADE API (FAPI) CONFORMANCE WALL
EO-SEC-06-009 
EO-SEC-06-010 ### Section 11.1: Establishment and Mandate of the FAPI Conformance Wall
EO-SEC-06-011 (a) There is hereby established the Financial-Grade API (FAPI) Conformance Wall, a mandatory cryptographic
EO-SEC-06-012 security boundary governing all digital financial transactions, data exchanges, and API endpoints operated
EO-SEC-06-013 by federal agencies, federally insured depository institutions, and designated systemic market utilities.
EO-SEC-06-014 (b) All covered entities shall transition their external and inter-agency financial interfaces to comply
EO-SEC-06-015 strictly with the Financial-Grade API (FAPI) 1.0 Advanced Security Profile and the FAPI 2.0 Security Profile,
EO-SEC-06-016 as maintained by the OpenID Foundation, within twelve (12) months of the date of this order.
EO-SEC-06-017 (c) No financial transaction or sensitive data exchange shall bypass the Conformance Wall. Any interface
EO-SEC-06-018 failing to meet these cryptographic standards shall be automatically restricted from accessing federal
EO-SEC-06-019 clearing systems, settlement networks, and public treasury data feeds.
EO-SEC-06-020 
EO-SEC-06-021 ### Section 11.2: Cryptographic and Protocol Specifications
EO-SEC-06-022 (a) To satisfy the requirements of the FAPI Conformance Wall, all API endpoints must implement:
EO-SEC-06-023   (i) Mutual Transport Layer Security (mTLS) conforming to RFC 8705, or Private Key JWT client authentication
EO-SEC-06-024       conforming to RFC 7523, utilizing asymmetric keys with a minimum strength of RSA 2048-bit or ECDSA P-256.
EO-SEC-06-025   (ii) Sender-constrained access tokens to prevent token leakage and unauthorized replay attacks, enforced
EO-SEC-06-026        via mTLS token binding or OAuth 2.0 Demonstrating Proof-of-Possession (DPoP) as specified in RFC 9449.
EO-SEC-06-027   (iii) JSON Web Token (JWT) Secured Authorization Response Mode (JARM) to guarantee the integrity and
EO-SEC-06-028         authenticity of authorization responses, preventing authorization code injection and state tampering.
EO-SEC-06-029   (iv) Strict asymmetric signing algorithms, specifically restricting allowed algorithms to PS256, ES256,
EO-SEC-06-030        or superior quantum-resistant algorithms as approved by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).
EO-SEC-06-031 (b) The use of static API keys, basic authentication, or unencrypted bearer tokens for any financial or
EO-SEC-06-032 high-value administrative transaction is strictly prohibited across all federal and regulated systems.
EO-SEC-06-033 
EO-SEC-06-034 ### Section 11.3: Compliance Auditing and Automated Enforcement
EO-SEC-06-035 (a) The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), in coordination with the Federal Financial
EO-SEC-06-036 Institutions Examination Council (FFIEC), shall deploy automated compliance verification suites.
EO-SEC-06-037 (b) These suites shall continuously execute active and passive cryptographic audits against all public-facing
EO-SEC-06-038 and partner-facing financial APIs to verify real-time adherence to FAPI conformance profiles.
EO-SEC-06-039 (c) Non-compliant endpoints shall receive an automated warning, followed by a mandatory revocation of
EO-SEC-06-040 cryptographic trust certificates within forty-eight (48) hours if remediation is not completed.
EO-SEC-06-041 
EO-SEC-06-042 ## PART II: MACHINE-TO-MACHINE (M2M) SETTLEMENT EQUIVALENCE
EO-SEC-06-043 
EO-SEC-06-044 ### Section 12.1: Legal Recognition of Autonomous Machine-to-Machine Transactions
EO-SEC-06-045 (a) It is the policy of the United States to foster a secure, automated, and friction-free digital economy.
EO-SEC-06-046 To this end, autonomous transactions executed between machine agents, smart contracts, and algorithmic
EO-SEC-06-047 systems are hereby declared to possess full legal equivalence to transactions executed by human agents.
EO-SEC-06-048 (b) A contract, settlement, or obligation may not be denied legal effect, validity, or enforceability
EO-SEC-06-049 solely because it was negotiated, formed, executed, or settled by autonomous machine-to-machine (M2M)
EO-SEC-06-050 systems operating without direct human intervention or real-time manual oversight.
EO-SEC-06-051 (c) Cryptographic signatures, zero-knowledge proofs, and state transitions generated by authorized M2M
EO-SEC-06-052 systems shall serve as definitive, legally binding evidence of intent, consent, and execution.
EO-SEC-06-053 
EO-SEC-06-054 ### Section 12.2: Aethel Syntax Legal Equivalence
EO-SEC-06-055 (a) The "Aethel" declarative syntax—a formal, mathematically verifiable specification language for smart
EO-SEC-06-056 contracts and state-machine transitions—is hereby recognized as a legally binding medium for contract
EO-SEC-06-057 representation and execution within the jurisdiction of the United States.
EO-SEC-06-058 (b) Any smart contract compiled, verified, and executed using valid Aethel syntax shall be interpreted
EO-SEC-06-059 by federal courts, regulatory bodies, and administrative agencies in accordance with its mathematical
EO-SEC-06-060 semantics. The compiled execution state of an Aethel contract shall constitute the "sole source of truth"
EO-SEC-06-061 regarding the performance, breach, or completion of the underlying legal agreement.
EO-SEC-06-062 (c) To qualify for legal equivalence under this section, an Aethel syntax contract must:
EO-SEC-06-063   (i) Be formally verified against a declared schema to prove the absence of reentrancy, integer overflow,
EO-SEC-06-064       and unhandled exception vulnerabilities.
EO-SEC-06-065   (ii) Contain an embedded, human-readable natural language preamble mapping the mathematical parameters
EO-SEC-06-066        of the code to corresponding legal intents, rights, and remedies.
EO-SEC-06-067   (iii) Be cryptographically signed by the public keys of all participating parties or their authorized
EO-SEC-06-068         autonomous machine delegates, registered under a FAPI-compliant identity provider.
EO-SEC-06-069 
EO-SEC-06-070 ### Section 12.3: Algorithmic Settlement and Clearing
EO-SEC-06-071 (a) Regulated financial institutions are authorized to establish automated, real-time clearing and settlement
EO-SEC-06-072 systems utilizing Aethel-compliant smart contracts. These systems may execute bilateral and multilateral
EO-SEC-06-073 netting, collateral management, and asset transfers autonomously.
EO-SEC-06-074 (b) The Federal Reserve and the Department of the Treasury shall establish gateway interfaces that allow
EO-SEC-06-075 Aethel-based M2M systems to settle directly in central bank liabilities or authorized digital currencies,
EO-SEC-06-076 provided that such systems operate behind the FAPI Conformance Wall.
EO-SEC-06-077 (c) Real-time algorithmic settlements executed under this framework shall be final and irrevocable upon
EO-SEC-06-078 the cryptographic commitment of the transaction to the underlying verified ledger or state machine.
EO-SEC-06-079 
EO-SEC-06-080 ## PART III: INTEGRATION AND INTEROPERABILITY
EO-SEC-06-081 
EO-SEC-06-082 ### Section 13.1: Unified Security and Legal Architecture
EO-SEC-06-083 (a) The FAPI Conformance Wall and the Aethel M2M Settlement Equivalence framework shall operate as a unified
EO-SEC-06-084 security and legal architecture. No autonomous M2M settlement system shall be granted legal equivalence
EO-SEC-06-085 unless its communication channels, identity assertions, and API endpoints are fully compliant with the
EO-SEC-06-086 FAPI Conformance Wall standards set forth in Part I of this order.
EO-SEC-06-087 (b) Conversely, any FAPI-compliant interface facilitating financial transactions must support the ingestion
EO-SEC-06-088 and processing of Aethel-structured payloads to ensure seamless, end-to-end cryptographic verification.
EO-SEC-06-089 
EO-SEC-06-090 ### Section 13.2: Dispute Resolution and Safe Harbor
EO-SEC-06-091 (a) In the event of a conflict between the mathematical execution of an Aethel contract and the natural
EO-SEC-06-092 language preamble, a designated cryptographic arbitration oracle, operating under FAPI-compliant security,
EO-SEC-06-093 shall be utilized to resolve the dispute according to pre-defined algorithmic rules.
EO-SEC-06-094 (b) A safe harbor is hereby established for financial institutions and technology providers who execute
EO-SEC-06-095 transactions in good faith using FAPI-conforming APIs and formally verified Aethel smart contracts.
EO-SEC-06-096 Such entities shall be immune from civil liability arising from systemic software anomalies, provided
EO-SEC-06-097 they have maintained continuous compliance with the auditing requirements of Section 11.3.
EO-SEC-06-098 
EO-SEC-06-099 ### Section 13.3: Definitions
EO-SEC-06-100 For the purposes of this order:
EO-SEC-06-101 (a) "Financial-Grade API (FAPI)" means the specifications developed by the OpenID Foundation designed to
EO-SEC-06-102     provide high-security OAuth profiles for protecting sensitive financial APIs.
EO-SEC-06-103 (b) "Aethel Syntax" means the formal, declarative, and mathematically verifiable domain-specific language
EO-SEC-06-104     used to define state transitions, smart contracts, and automated legal agreements.
EO-SEC-06-105 (c) "Machine-to-Machine (M2M) Settlement" means the automated transfer of value, assets, or data between
EO-SEC-06-106     two or more autonomous computational agents without manual human intervention.
EO-SEC-06-107 (d) "Sender-Constrained Token" means a cryptographic token that is bound to a specific client's private key,
EO-SEC-06-108     rendering it unusable if intercepted by an unauthorized third party.
