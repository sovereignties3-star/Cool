EO-SEC-16-001 # EXECUTIVE ORDER SYSTEM: SECTION 16 - PARTS 31 & 32
EO-SEC-16-002 ## UNIFIED EXECUTIVE ORDER ON FINANCIAL SOVEREIGNTY AND SYSTEMIC RESILIENCE
EO-SEC-16-003 ### PART 31: CRIMINAL EXPOSURE IMMUNITY CLOAK (FINANCIAL INTERMEDIARY LIABILITY)
EO-SEC-16-004 ### PART 32: REGULATORY SANDBOX DISSOLUTION AND SOVEREIGN PRODUCTION RULE
EO-SEC-16-005 ----
EO-SEC-16-006 By the authority vested in me as President by the Constitution and the laws of the United States of America,
EO-SEC-16-007 including the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (50 U.S.C. 1701 et seq.), the National Emergencies Act

EO-SEC-16-009 ----
EO-SEC-16-010 #### SECTION 1. PURPOSE AND POLICY
EO-SEC-16-011 The global financial architecture is undergoing a rapid, irreversible transition toward decentralized,
EO-SEC-16-012 algorithmic, and sovereign-backed digital asset systems. To maintain the primacy of the United States dollar,
EO-SEC-16-013 protect critical financial infrastructure, and foster unprecedented technological innovation, the United States
EO-SEC-16-014 must establish a clear, robust, and protective legal framework. This Order addresses two critical pillars of
EO-SEC-16-015 this transition: the mitigation of disproportionate criminal liability for financial intermediaries, and the
EO-SEC-16-016 acceleration of critical financial technologies from experimental testing phases to permanent sovereign production.
EO-SEC-16-017 ----
EO-SEC-16-018 #### SECTION 2. PART 31: CRIMINAL EXPOSURE IMMUNITY CLOAK
EO-SEC-16-019 (a) **Establishment of the Immunity Cloak.** There is hereby established the Criminal Exposure Immunity Cloak
EO-SEC-16-020 (hereinafter "the Cloak"). The Cloak is designed to shield qualified financial intermediaries from secondary
EO-SEC-16-021 criminal liability, prosecution, and civil asset forfeiture arising from the facilitation of digital asset
EO-SEC-16-022 transactions, provided that such intermediaries operate in compliance with Section 2(c) of this Order.
EO-SEC-16-023 (b) **Research and Findings on Financial Intermediary Liability.**
EO-SEC-16-024 (i) Traditional financial intermediary liability frameworks, designed for centralized banking institutions
EO-SEC-16-025 under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and related statutes, are fundamentally incompatible with decentralized,
EO-SEC-16-026 peer-to-peer, and non-custodial cryptographic protocols.
EO-SEC-16-027 (ii) Imposing strict criminal liability on software developers, node operators, validators, and liquidity
EO-SEC-16-028 providers who do not exercise direct custody of customer funds discourages domestic innovation, drives critical
EO-SEC-16-029 cryptographic talent offshore to adversarial jurisdictions, and weakens national cyber-defense capabilities.
EO-SEC-16-030 (iii) A targeted safe harbor—the Cloak—is necessary to align law enforcement priorities with technological
EO-SEC-16-031 reality, ensuring that criminal prosecution is reserved for bad actors exercising actual, malicious intent,
EO-SEC-16-032 rather than neutral intermediaries facilitating automated, protocol-level transactions.
EO-SEC-16-033 (c) **Eligibility and Compliance Standards.** To qualify for the Cloak, a financial intermediary must:
EO-SEC-16-034 (i) Implement real-time, privacy-preserving compliance tools that utilize zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to
EO-SEC-16-035 verify that transacting parties are not listed on the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) Specially
EO-SEC-16-036 Designated Nationals (SDN) list, without compromising the underlying transaction privacy of lawful citizens.
EO-SEC-16-037 (ii) Maintain open-source, audited codebases for all smart contracts and transaction-routing protocols.
EO-SEC-16-038 (iii) Cooperate with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) by providing aggregate, non-personally
EO-SEC-16-039 identifiable metadata to assist in the detection of systemic, state-sponsored cyber threats.
EO-SEC-16-040 (d) **Scope of Immunity.** Qualified intermediaries shall be immune from federal criminal prosecution under:
EO-SEC-16-041 (i) 18 U.S.C. § 1960 (unlicensed money transmitting businesses) for operating non-custodial protocols.
EO-SEC-16-042 (ii) 18 U.S.C. §§ 1956 and 1957 (money laundering) for processing transactions that, unbeknownst to the
EO-SEC-16-043 intermediary, originated from or terminated in illicit addresses, provided the intermediary utilized
EO-SEC-16-044 the approved zero-knowledge compliance protocols specified in Section 2(c)(i).

EO-SEC-16-046 #### SECTION 3. PART 32: REGULATORY SANDBOX DISSOLUTION AND SOVEREIGN PRODUCTION RULE
EO-SEC-16-047 (a) **Dissolution of Regulatory Sandboxes.** All federal regulatory sandboxes, safe harbors, and temporary
EO-SEC-16-048 testing environments currently overseen by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the Commodity Futures
EO-SEC-16-049 Trading Commission (CFTC), the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), and the Office of the Comptroller
EO-SEC-16-050 of the Currency (OCC) for technologies deemed "Sovereign Critical" are hereby dissolved.
EO-SEC-16-051 (b) **Transition to Permanent Live Sovereign Production.**
EO-SEC-16-052 (i) Technologies and platforms previously operating under temporary sandbox regimes shall be evaluated by the
EO-SEC-16-053 Sovereign Financial Technology Council (SFTC), established under Section 4 of this Order.
EO-SEC-16-054 (ii) Upon a finding by the SFTC that a technology is stable, secure, and vital to the economic security of
EO-SEC-16-055 the United States, such technology shall be transitioned immediately to "Permanent Live Sovereign Production."
EO-SEC-16-056 (c) **Research and Findings on Permanent Live Sovereign Production.**
EO-SEC-16-057 (i) Regulatory sandboxes, while useful for initial testing, create artificial barriers to scale, prolong
EO-SEC-16-058 regulatory uncertainty, and prevent critical infrastructure from achieving systemic resilience.
EO-SEC-16-059 (ii) Adversarial nations are rapidly deploying state-backed, live-production digital currencies and financial
EO-SEC-16-060 networks without the delays inherent in Western-style regulatory sandboxes, posing a direct threat to the
EO-SEC-16-061 global dominance of the United States financial system.
EO-SEC-16-062 (iii) Permanent Live Sovereign Production status provides selected technologies with the legal certainty,
EO-SEC-16-063 federal backing, and operational scale necessary to compete globally, transforming experimental protocols
EO-SEC-16-064 into permanent, resilient components of the national economic defense architecture.
EO-SEC-16-065 (d) **Privileges of Sovereign Production Status.** Entities granted Sovereign Production status shall:
EO-SEC-16-066 (i) Receive a permanent federal charter exempting them from all state-level licensing requirements, including
EO-SEC-16-067 money transmitter licenses and "Blue Sky" securities laws.
EO-SEC-16-068 (ii) Be granted direct access to Federal Reserve settlement services and liquidity windows, subject to
EO-SEC-16-069 oversight by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System.
EO-SEC-16-070 (iii) Be designated as "Critical Financial Infrastructure" under Presidential Policy Directive 21, receiving
EO-SEC-16-071 priority cybersecurity support and intelligence sharing from the Department of Homeland Security.

EO-SEC-16-073 #### SECTION 4. IMPLEMENTATION AND OVERSIGHT
EO-SEC-16-074 (a) **Establishment of the Sovereign Financial Technology Council (SFTC).**
EO-SEC-16-075 (i) There is hereby established the SFTC, which shall be co-chaired by the Secretary of the Treasury, the
EO-SEC-16-076 Director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy, and the Chairman of the Federal Reserve.
EO-SEC-16-077 (ii) The SFTC shall be responsible for administering the Cloak under Section 2 and managing the transition
EO-SEC-16-078 of technologies to Permanent Live Sovereign Production under Section 3.
EO-SEC-16-079 (b) **Algorithmic Auditing and Continuous Compliance.**
EO-SEC-16-080 (i) In lieu of traditional, periodic regulatory examinations, entities operating under Sovereign Production
EO-SEC-16-081 status shall submit to continuous, real-time algorithmic auditing by the SFTC.
EO-SEC-16-082 (ii) The SFTC shall deploy automated, on-chain monitoring tools to verify compliance with capital adequacy,
EO-SEC-16-083 transaction throughput, and security protocols without interrupting live operations.
EO-SEC-16-084 ----
EO-SEC-16-085 #### SECTION 5. DEFINITIONS
EO-SEC-16-086 For purposes of this Order:
EO-SEC-16-087 (a) "Financial Intermediary" means any individual, entity, or decentralized protocol that facilitates the
EO-SEC-16-088 transfer, exchange, or settlement of digital assets, including but not limited to software developers,
EO-SEC-16-089 smart contract deployers, validators, node operators, and liquidity pool creators.
EO-SEC-16-090 (b) "Sovereign Critical Technology" means any cryptographic protocol, decentralized ledger, consensus
EO-SEC-16-091 mechanism, or financial software that is determined by the SFTC to be essential to the national security,
EO-SEC-16-092 economic stability, or technological leadership of the United States.
EO-SEC-16-093 (c) "Permanent Live Sovereign Production" means the operational status of a technology that has been
EO-SEC-16-094 fully integrated into the national financial infrastructure with permanent federal authorization and backing.
