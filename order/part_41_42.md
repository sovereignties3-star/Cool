EO-SEC-21-001 # EXECUTIVE ORDER: UNIFIED DIGITAL ASSET AND TECHNICAL LITERACY FRAMEWORK
EO-SEC-21-002 ---
EO-SEC-21-003 ## PART 41: COMMODITY MATRIX UNIFICATION (SEC-CFTC JURISDICTIONAL HARMONIZATION)
EO-SEC-21-004 
EO-SEC-21-005 ### Section 4101: Purpose and Scope of the Unified Commodity Matrix
EO-SEC-21-006 (a) This section establishes a unified regulatory matrix to resolve the historical jurisdictional ambiguities between the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) regarding digital assets. By synthesizing the Howey Test criteria with the Commodity Exchange Act (CEA) standards, this mandate creates a clear, algorithmic classification system for digital assets.
EO-SEC-21-007 (b) The lack of regulatory clarity has historically impeded domestic innovation, driven capital offshore, and compromised consumer protection. This Part provides a definitive, legally binding framework to categorize digital assets as either digital securities, digital commodities, or hybrid instruments.
EO-SEC-21-008 
EO-SEC-21-009 ### Section 4102: Algorithmic Classification and Decentralization Metrics
EO-SEC-21-010 (a) The Joint SEC-CFTC Digital Asset Working Group (DAWG) shall implement a quantitative "Decentralization Index" (DI) to determine asset classification.
EO-SEC-21-011 (b) Assets scoring above the threshold of 0.75 on the DI, which measures node distribution, governance concentration, and developer control, shall be classified as Digital Commodities under the exclusive jurisdiction of the CFTC.
EO-SEC-21-012 (c) Assets scoring below 0.75 shall remain classified as Digital Investment Contracts (Securities) under the jurisdiction of the SEC, subject to a path to decentralization safe harbor.
EO-SEC-21-013 (d) The DI shall be calculated using the following parameters:
EO-SEC-21-014     (1) Gini Coefficient of Token Distribution: A mathematical representation of token wealth distribution among addresses.
EO-SEC-21-015     (2) Nakamoto Coefficient: The minimum number of entities that can collectively control or disrupt the consensus mechanism.
EO-SEC-21-016     (3) Developer Concentration: The ratio of commits and protocol modifications executed by core developers versus independent contributors.
EO-SEC-21-017     (4) Governance Power: The distribution of voting rights or protocol upgrade keys among non-affiliated entities.
EO-SEC-21-018 
EO-SEC-21-019 ### Section 4103: Interagency Data Sharing and Unified Registry
EO-SEC-21-020 (a) A single, shared registry of all classified digital assets shall be maintained in real-time, accessible to both agencies and the public.
EO-SEC-21-021 (b) This registry will eliminate duplicative filing requirements and streamline compliance for market participants.
EO-SEC-21-022 (c) The registry shall be hosted on a secure, permissioned federal ledger to ensure data integrity, immutability, and real-time synchronization between the SEC and CFTC databases.
EO-SEC-21-023 
EO-SEC-21-024 ### Section 4104: Transition and Safe Harbor Protocols
EO-SEC-21-025 (a) A "Safe Harbor" period of 18 months is hereby established for nascent digital asset projects to achieve the decentralization threshold of 0.75 on the DI.
EO-SEC-21-026 (b) During this safe harbor period, the SEC shall suspend unilateral enforcement actions against projects that have filed a "Notice of Intent to Decentralize" with the DAWG, provided they submit semi-annual progress reports.
EO-SEC-21-027 (c) If a project fails to meet the decentralization threshold within the safe harbor period, it may apply for a single 6-month extension, subject to demonstrating substantial progress toward governance distribution.

EO-SEC-21-029 ## PART 42: TECHNICAL LITERACY MANDATE AND CONGRESSIONAL VERIFICATION
EO-SEC-21-030 
EO-SEC-21-031 ### Section 4201: Establishment of the Congressional Technical Verification Office (CTVO)
EO-SEC-21-032 (a) To ensure that federal regulations are grounded in modern technological realities, there is hereby established the Congressional Technical Verification Office (CTVO), modeled after the legacy Office of Technology Assessment (OTA).
EO-SEC-21-033 (b) The CTVO shall serve as a non-partisan, independent advisory body to Congress and federal regulatory agencies, tasked with evaluating the technical feasibility, security, and systemic impacts of proposed technology-related legislation and rules.
EO-SEC-21-034 (c) The CTVO shall be staffed by leading experts in cryptography, distributed systems, computer science, economics, and cybersecurity, recruited through competitive federal fellowships.
EO-SEC-21-035 
EO-SEC-21-036 ### Section 4202: Mandatory Technical Literacy Standards for Regulatory Personnel
EO-SEC-21-037 (a) All senior staff, commissioners, and policy advisors within the SEC, CFTC, and associated financial regulatory agencies must undergo bi-annual technical literacy training.
EO-SEC-21-038 (b) Curriculum must cover distributed ledger technology, cryptographic consensus mechanisms, smart contract auditing, and artificial intelligence governance.
EO-SEC-21-039 (c) The CTVO shall design and administer the certification exams. Failure to achieve a passing score within three attempts shall disqualify personnel from leading digital asset regulatory divisions.
EO-SEC-21-040 
EO-SEC-21-041 ### Section 4203: Technical Verification Protocols for Legislative and Regulatory Drafts
EO-SEC-21-042 (a) No regulatory framework or enforcement action concerning digital assets may be finalized without a "Technical Impact Statement" (TIS) certified by the CTVO.
EO-SEC-21-043 (b) The TIS must verify that the proposed rules are technologically feasible, do not inadvertently outlaw fundamental cryptographic primitives, and are neutral regarding specific software implementations.
EO-SEC-21-044 (c) The TIS shall evaluate:
EO-SEC-21-045     (1) Protocol Neutrality: Ensuring regulations do not favor proof-of-work over proof-of-stake, or vice versa, unless justified by clear national security or environmental metrics.
EO-SEC-21-046     (2) Cryptographic Privacy: Protecting the use of zero-knowledge proofs and other privacy-preserving technologies when used for legitimate, non-custodial transactions.
EO-SEC-21-047     (3) Smart Contract Liability: Clarifying that software developers are not liable for the unauthorized use of open-source code by third parties, provided they do not retain administrative control over the deployed protocol.
EO-SEC-21-048 
EO-SEC-21-049 ### Section 4204: Funding and Interagency Cooperation
EO-SEC-21-050 (a) The CTVO shall be funded through a 0.05% surcharge on digital asset registration fees collected under the Unified Commodity Matrix registry.
EO-SEC-21-051 (b) All executive departments and agencies shall cooperate fully with the CTVO, providing access to technical data, proprietary regulatory models, and personnel as requested for verification purposes.
