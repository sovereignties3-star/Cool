EO-SEC-14-001: # EXECUTIVE ORDER: UNIFIED DIGITAL SOVEREIGNTY AND COMPLIANCE DIRECTIVE
EO-SEC-14-002: ## SUBCHAPTER XIV: INTEGRATION OF LEGISLATIVE INTERCEPTS AND ENTERPRISE MANDATES
EO-SEC-14-003: ### PART 27: GENERAL ORDERS INTERCEPT RIDER (SENATE CALENDAR SYNCHRONIZATION)
EO-SEC-14-004: #### Section 2701. Establishment of the Legislative Intercept Framework
EO-SEC-14-005: (a) The President of the United States, pursuant to Article II of the Constitution and statutory authorities vested under the National Emergencies Act (50 U.S.C. 1601 et seq.) and the Defense Production Act (50 U.S.C. 4501 et seq.), hereby directs the establishment of the General Orders Intercept Rider (GOIR) system.
EO-SEC-14-006: (b) The GOIR system shall actively monitor the Senate Legislative Calendar of Business, specifically tracking measures placed under "General Orders" pursuant to Senate Rule VIII, to identify legislative tracks that may impact national security, digital infrastructure, or federal procurement.
EO-SEC-14-007: (c) The Office of Management and Budget (OMB), in coordination with the Office of Legislative Affairs (OLA) and the National Security Council (NSC), shall analyze all bills, resolutions, and joint resolutions on the Calendar to identify provisions that intersect with federal cybersecurity and technology mandates.
EO-SEC-14-008: #### Section 2702. Senate Legislative Calendar Procedures and Intercept Protocols
EO-SEC-14-009: (a) To prevent conflicting regulatory frameworks and ensure seamless policy execution, the Executive Branch shall execute strategic administrative interventions when a bill on the Senate Calendar of Business approaches a motion to proceed or is scheduled for consideration by unanimous consent.
EO-SEC-14-010: (b) The Intercept Protocol requires the National Security Advisor, in consultation with the Director of CISA, to issue an Administrative Impact Statement (AIS) to the President of the Senate, the Senate Majority Leader, and the Senate Minority Leader within forty-eight (48) hours of any measure being placed on the Executive Calendar or the Calendar of Business.
EO-SEC-14-011: (c) The AIS shall detail the technical compatibility of the pending legislation with the public technology stack integration standards defined under Part 28 of this Order, highlighting any potential security vulnerabilities or operational redundancies.
EO-SEC-14-012: (d) If a legislative measure is identified as incompatible, the President shall exercise constitutional authorities to align executive agency procurement rules, thereby establishing a de facto operational standard that preempts administrative friction prior to final legislative passage.
EO-SEC-14-013: #### Section 2703. Reporting and Legislative Liaison Coordination
EO-SEC-14-014: (a) The Director of the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) shall submit weekly briefs to the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs and the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation.
EO-SEC-14-015: (b) These briefs must cross-reference all active Senate Calendar items with the compliance status of the 1,200 designated technology companies under Part 28 of this Order.
EO-SEC-14-016: (c) This synchronization ensures that legislative policy is informed by real-time telemetry derived from the public technology stack integration pipelines, creating a continuous feedback loop between legislative intent and executive execution.
EO-SEC-14-017: ### PART 28: 1,200 TECH COMPANY COMPLIANCE MANDATE (PUBLIC STACK INTEGRATION)
EO-SEC-14-018: #### Section 2801. Designation of Covered Technology Providers
EO-SEC-14-019: (a) The Secretary of Commerce, in consultation with the Director of CISA and the Director of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), shall maintain a registry of the one thousand two hundred (1,200) largest technology companies operating within United States jurisdiction, determined by market capitalization, data volume, and critical infrastructure dependency.
EO-SEC-14-020: (b) These designated entities, hereinafter referred to as "Covered Technology Providers," are critical to the economic security, national defense, and public health of the United States.
EO-SEC-14-021: (c) All Covered Technology Providers shall be subject to the mandatory integration protocols established under Section 2802 of this Part.
EO-SEC-14-022: #### Section 2802. Public Technology Stack Integration Standards
EO-SEC-14-023: (a) Covered Technology Providers must integrate their core software, cloud infrastructure, and data pipelines with the Federal Public Technology Stack (FPTS) to ensure interoperability, security, and real-time threat intelligence sharing.
EO-SEC-14-024: (b) The FPTS integration requires strict adherence to the following technical specifications:
EO-SEC-14-025:   (1) Implementation of standardized, open-source API endpoints for real-time telemetry and threat reporting.
EO-SEC-14-026:   (2) Mandatory adoption of FedRAMP High baseline security controls for all cloud services and infrastructure.
EO-SEC-14-027:   (3) Integration of zero-trust architecture utilizing OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect (OIDC) for identity verification.
EO-SEC-14-028:   (4) Deployment of gRPC-based communication protocols for low-latency, secure data synchronization.
EO-SEC-14-029: (c) Non-compliance with these integration standards within one hundred and eighty (180) days of designation shall result in immediate suspension of federal procurement eligibility and potential civil penalties.
EO-SEC-14-030: #### Section 2803. Enforcement, Auditing, and Penalties
EO-SEC-14-031: (a) The Department of Justice (DOJ), in coordination with the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), shall establish a joint task force to audit compliance with the FPTS mandates.
EO-SEC-14-032: (b) Audits shall be conducted bi-annually, utilizing automated compliance verification tools and independent third-party assessors.
EO-SEC-14-033: (c) Civil penalties for non-compliance shall be assessed at up to five percent (5%) of the Covered Technology Provider's global gross revenue for each day of continued violation.
EO-SEC-14-034: (d) The Attorney General is authorized to seek injunctive relief in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia to compel immediate integration of the public stack.
EO-SEC-14-035: ### PART 29: ADMINISTRATIVE PROVISIONS AND SEVERABILITY
EO-SEC-14-036: #### Section 2901. Implementation Timeline and Funding
EO-SEC-14-037: (a) The provisions of Part 27 and Part 28 shall take effect immediately upon signature.
EO-SEC-14-038: (b) Funding for the implementation of the GOIR and FPTS systems shall be allocated from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency's annual operating budget and the Technology Modernization Fund.
EO-SEC-14-039: #### Section 2902. Severability
EO-SEC-14-040: If any provision of this Order, or the application of such provision to any person or circumstance, is held to be invalid, the remainder of this Order and the application of its provisions to other persons or circumstances shall not be affected thereby.
